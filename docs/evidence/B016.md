## Format: a sibling of the text pack, kept minimal

The text pack (specs/23 §4) is keyed by Bible book, chapter and verse: `books[].chapters[]` of
`first_verse..last_verse`, chunks of `ChapterVerse`. The *Institutes* has none of these. Its places
are `book.chapter.section` locators of its own scheme, and its front matter has no number at all.
A section is a run of paragraphs (1,456 paragraphs in 1,288 sections) that carries notes. Forcing
it into verse rows would invent a Bible-shaped coordinate the B068 grammar does not have.

specs/19 already says a work ships as "one read-only SQLite file per edition or work" with text,
FTS5, edges and a `section_map`. So the work pack is that pack, schema `b068-1` unchanged. The
engine, the catalog, the link index and the edition search read it like any pack. It adds only
what reading by section needs: three manifest keys (`work_format`, `work_info`, `work_structure`)
and three tables (`work_section`, `work_citation`, `cite_span`). `text.verse_id` is the
section's ordinal in reading order, and `body` holds its paragraphs joined by a blank line. A
browser fetches one file (8,994,816 bytes; 2,935,835 with gzip -9).

## Decisions about the text

Each is a line of code a reviewer can change. A change gives a new `content_revision`.

1. **Kept as printed:** every scalar between the digitizer's start and end lines, except what
   2 to 6 list. Italics stay as the digitizer marked them (`_cultus_`; volume II's transcriber
   says so, and volume I uses the same convention). The translator's bracketed words, Greek,
   Latin and the U+2010 hyphens stay. Nothing is normalized.
2. **Paragraphs and line breaks.** The files are CR LF (69,897 lines); the CR is the line end,
   not text. A paragraph is a run of non-blank lines, and its lines are joined with one space
   (49,846 joins). The exception is a line ending in U+2010 HYPHEN, which joins with nothing (13
   joins, all compounds: `self‐existence`, `judgment‐seat`, `free‐woman`, `co‐heirs`, `first‐born`,
   `sweet‐smelling`, `non‐imputation`, `hand‐writing`, `self‐excuse`, `self‐gratulation`,
   `self‐denial`). Neither file breaks a word with an ASCII hyphen at a line end. The digitizer
   keeps em dashes tight (`two—the`); none ends a line inside a paragraph.
   **A paragraph whose every line is indented is a block** (39: the Dedication's salutation,
   verse quotations, the centred lines of the title pages and Book IV's argument). A block keeps
   its line breaks, with each line trimmed, because a verse's line breaks cannot be told from the
   digitizer's wrapping. Indentation depth is not carried. The other 1,402 paragraphs are prose.
3. **Note markers leave the text and become anchors.** Volume I prints `(61)`, volume II `[703]`.
   A marker is taken out only when it is the volume's next expected number, and it is kept on its
   note with the paragraph and scalar offset where it stood (3,506 markers). Both volumes' markers
   run 1–2016 and 1–1490 with no gap, repeat or stray number, and no double space is left behind.
   One double space comes from the source itself (volume II line 2900, reported).
4. **Notes are notes.** The volume I notes come from its `FOOTNOTES` block and the volume II notes
   from the `Footnote N:` blocks after each chapter. Each is joined like prose and kept whole on
   the section its marker is in. That holds for citation notes and for the translator's or
   editor's comments, so nothing is dropped and nothing is promoted into the running text. The
   kinds are 3,431 citations only, 31 comment with citations (mixed) and 44 comment only (the
   translator's preface's long note, the Latin sources of the Dedication, the classical
   references of Book I, and Greek quoted under a citation). Notes are not in the FTS index:
   search covers the running text. That is an open choice for the owner.
5. **Left out, with line counts in the report:** the digitizer's header (56 lines) and licence
   trailer (704). Also left out: volume I's table of contents (117 lines, the digitizer's, with a
   `Footnotes` entry the print does not have), the `[Cover Art]` line, the two transcriber's notes
   (below), and volume II's repeated `BOOK III.` heading. The two indexes at the end of volume II
   (4,301 lines) cite volume and page of the print edition, which the pack does not carry. The
   verifier uses the Scripture index as an outside witness instead (below).
6. **Headings inside a chapter** (15: the ten `The … Commandment.` headings of 2.8 and
   `CONFIRMATION.`, `PENANCE.`, `EXTREME UNCTION.`, `ECCLESIASTICAL ORDERS.`, `MATRIMONY.` of
   4.19) belong, with what follows them up to the next numbered section, to that next section.
   For example, 2.8.13 opens with the heading and the commandment's text, then `XIII.`.
7. **Sections.** A chapter's first section is not numbered in the source and becomes `N.N.1`.
   A prose paragraph that opens with a canonical roman numeral and `. ` inside a chapter starts the
   section it numbers. The number is taken as printed and checked against the expected next one.
   A gap, repeat or step backwards would be reported and never renumbered, and a second section
   with one locator stops the build. None occurs. The numbered paragraphs of the General Syllabus
   and of Book IV's argument are not sections, because they are not in a chapter.
8. **Front matter and arguments are places too**, outside the B068 grammar: `title.1`,
   `advertisement`, `translator-preface`, `author-preface`, `dedication` (the Epistle to King
   Francis I), `syllabus`, `title.2` (volume II's title page with the Buchanan epigraph, placed
   after volume I's front matter), and `1.argument` … `4.argument`. They get no `section_map`
   row, because they are not citable as `Inst.`.

## Rights, licence and attribution

The B071 gate passed for S75: status `approved`, `local_read` and `public_index` allowed,
`MANIFEST.sha256` = `e2d5df9a…24f6` = the record's `artifact_sha256` = `rights/S75/manifest.sha256`,
and all four listed files hash as listed (4 = `acquisition.file_count`, licence files in the
manifest).

The digitizer's licence, as both files print it:

- **1.C**: the Foundation does "not claim a right to prevent you from copying, distributing,
  performing, displaying or creating derivative works based on the work as long as all references
  to Project Gutenberg are removed."
- **1.E**: "Unless you have removed all references to Project Gutenberg:" paragraphs 1.E.1–1.E.9
  apply (the 1.E.1 sentence, the full licence, the trademark terms).

**The lead's ruling, a conservative reading of 1.C and 1.E, open for the steward to relax:**
nothing that ships in the pack refers to the digitizer. Its provenance stays in the repository's
rights record (`rights/S75.json`) and in the build report beside the pack, which never ships.
Before the ruling, the pack carried the record's attribution verbatim, which names Project
Gutenberg; I raised that with the lead during the work.

- **Text.** The header (title block, release line, start line) and the trailer (end line,
  licence, trademark text) are cut before parsing. The `[Transcriber’s Note]`, which names
  Distributed Proofreaders, is left out with the others.
- **Attribution and metadata.** The manifest's `attribution`, `license` and `source`, and
  `work_info`, carry no reference to the digitizer. The attribution is the constant
  `PACK_ATTRIBUTION`, built from the work's own title page: "John Calvin, Institutes of the
  Christian Religion, translated by John Allen. Sixth American Edition, Revised and Corrected.
  Philadelphia: Presbyterian Board of Publication. Public domain in the United States." The
  licence field is `LicenseRef-public-domain-US`, and the source is "rights record S75".
- **What stays outside the pack.** The rights record's own sentence and licence identifier both
  name PG. They are copied into the report (`rights_attribution`), together with each volume's
  eBook number, release and update dates, credits and file SHA-256 (`digitized`).
- **Every output byte.** The build reads the finished pack file and refuses it if any byte
  sequence matches "gutenberg" case-insensitively (`names_the_digitizer`).
  - Tests: a crafted buffer is caught in any case, and the pack's constants pass. The parser's
    test volumes, which carry the digitizer's header and end lines, are written through the
    whole writer, and no byte of the result names it.
  - The second reader checks the raw file the same way.
  - On the built pack, `grep -aic gutenberg` gives 0, and so do `pg45001`, `pg64392`, `ebook`,
    `pgdp` and "distributed proofread". `45001` appears only inside verse ids such as
    `45001010` (Acts 1:10).

## Anomalies of the source

None is repaired. The report (`institutes-allen.json`, `findings`) lists every one.

- **Section numbering: no gap, repeat or misnumbering** in any of the 80 chapters. The second
  reader counts the numbered paragraphs of each chapter on its own and agrees on all 80.
- **Note numbering:** markers and notes both run 1–2016 (volume I) and 1–1490 (volume II), each
  marker once, in order, every one with its note.
- **One double space** in volume II's text at line 2900, as printed.
- **Misprints the citations show** (kept as printed; the link follows what is printed):
  `Exod. xxxviii. 35` (Exodus 38 has 31 verses), `Heb. ix. 26; x. 10; xiv. 18` (Hebrews has 13
  chapters), `Like viii. 5-15` (for Luke), and `Ez. xxxiii. 14, 15`, which could be Ezra or Ezekiel
  by its abbreviation. Note 1:61, under "lives and moves" in 1.1.1, prints `Acts xvii. 2.`
  where the quotation is Acts 17:28. It resolves to what it prints, Acts 17:2. Such a misprint
  cannot be told from a deliberate citation without a guess, so the link follows the print.
- **Dates:** the volume I title page prints `1813` under the Sixth American Edition. The
  Translator's Preface is signed "LONDON, _May 12, 1813_.", which dates the translation.
  Volume II's Scripture index is dated MDCCCLII (1852) by its compiler S. T. Livermore, so this
  printing is no earlier than 1852 and is not dated in the source. The catalog keeps these
  apart: `title_page_date` 1813, `translation_date` 1813 (citing the preface's signature),
  `printing_date` null, and `original_date` 1559, the author's final Latin edition, which the
  author's preface heading names.
- **Acts xvii. 2:** this is a probable misprint, and it is kept and linked as printed (see
  Citations).

## Scripture citations

`crates/preview-pack/src/institutes/citations.rs` finds where a citation starts and ends in a
note and rewrites its numbers for the core parser: `Gen. xviii. 2; xxxii. 1, 28` becomes
`Gen. 18:2; 32:1, 28`. The **core reference parser** (`openlogos_core::reference::parse`) decides
what the citation means, with the English alias table plus nine 1813 spellings: Ephes, Numb,
Eccles, Psal, Philip, Jerem, Hebr, Coloss and Lament. A resolved citation must exist in `eng`
(the canon table). Its ranges, cut at chapter ends, become one `explicitly_cites` edge each, and
they are also stored in `cite_span` for the reverse lookup.

Further things the rules hold to:

- **Commentary citations.** `Aug. in Psal. cxliv.` and `In Psalm cxiii.` (1.5.9, 1.11.9, 1.11.10)
  cite Augustine's exposition *on* a Psalm. They resolve to the Psalm with the detail "cited as
  the subject of a commentary", so a host can tell them apart.
- **Chapter lists** (`John vii. 18. viii. 50.`, `Deut. x. 16, and xxx. 6.`, `Ps. xciii. xcvi.`)
  carry the book over. A bare numeral counts as a chapter only when the list ends or another
  numeral follows, so `i. e.` is never read as chapter 1.
- **Running text**: the body has no citation in the notes' form (no `Book roman. verse` in
  1.1–4.20 outside the notes), so only notes are mined.

**Second reading.** The verifier reads every note with its own regular expressions and book
table. It agrees with the pack on **3,504 of 3,506 notes**. The two that differ are the
`not_in_scheme` cases above, which the reader links naively and the pack correctly does not.

**Outside witness: the print edition's Scripture index** (Livermore, 1852, at the end of
volume II; left out of the reading text). It lists 3,875 entries over 2,566 distinct verses, and
**2,429 of them (94.7 %) are verses the pack links**. The pack links 2,910 distinct verses, and
83.5 % of those are in the index. The index counts citations in the running text too, and the
compiler's selection is his own, so full agreement is not expected. A broken citation reader would
show here.

## Search, catalog and the palette

- **Full text:** the pack's `text_fts` uses the shared `openlogos` tokenizer (`ol-norm-1`), like
  the editions. `EditionSearch` attaches it beside them. A hit names `institutes:3.2.7`, its
  snippet puts paragraph breaks back as spaces, and the verse-shaped `TextIndex` leaves it out.
- **Palette:** a host registers `institutes-allen` → `institutes` with `set_resource_schemes`,
  which is the same call that registers an edition's versification. A resource whose scheme is a
  work grammar's is then a work. `Inst. 3.2.7`, `Institutes III.ii.7`, `:inst 3.2.7` and
  `institutes:3.2.7` each give a Passage row, "Institutes 3.2.7" with the edition's short title,
  at 1000. A text hit gives a Text row, "Institutes (Allen) · Institutes 3.2.7". Both dispatch
  `tab.open_edition` with `resource_id` and `reference` = `institutes:3.2.7`, which opens the work
  beside the reader's passage. A column target (`@2`, `@new`) works as for passages. Tested in
  `palette.rs` and through the native `ShellHandle`.
- **Following:** a work tab opened from a Bible tab inherits its link state. A later Bible
  navigation would have written `Rom.8.28` into it, so navigation and scrolling now carry only tabs
  of the same family: Bible with Bible, one work's scheme with the same scheme. The test fails
  without the filter.
- **Catalog entry, offline** (`WorkInfo` in the pack's manifest): every fact comes from the
  work's own pages, and the build checks that each is printed where it is said to be or stops.
  - Volume I's title page: author John Calvin, translator John Allen, "Sixth American Edition,
    Revised and Corrected. In Two Volumes.", Philadelphia, Presbyterian Board of Publication,
    and the title-page date 1813. Volume II's title page gives the edition and place again.
  - The translation's date, 1813, comes from the Translator's Preface's signature.
  - The printing date is unknown and recorded as such, no earlier than 1852 by the bound index.
  - The original's date is 1559, the final Latin edition named in the author's preface heading.
  - None of it is taken from the digitizer's header or record page: an earlier version read
    author and translator from the record page, and it no longer does.
  - The entry also holds the attribution and licence above, the rights record id, and the counts.
- **B068 shapes:** each numbered section has a `section_map` row (`equal`, segment = locator),
  which makes 1,277 rows. The edges are `src` = (`institutes`, `3.2.7`), `rel` =
  `explicitly_cites`, `dst` = (`bible`, `46008028` or `first-last`), `extracted`,
  `source_attested`, method `b016-allen-notes@1 (core reference parser, grammar b068-1)`, and
  `context` = the citation as printed. These are B057's Bible locators. The link index installs
  them (tested), so `backlinks` on a single-verse locator lists the section. A verse inside a
  range is found through `cite_span`, as B057's `xref_range` does for cross references; the
  separate table keeps the two branches apart.

## Core API

`openlogos_retrieval_sqlite::work_pack::WorkPack`: `open(path)`, `from_bytes`, `info`,
`structure`, `canonical_locator`, `section`, `section_at`, `chapter`, `citations`,
`all_citations`, `cited_by(passage, scheme)` and `cited_count`. A passage in another scheme
(`org`) is mapped verse by verse into `eng` first. Bindings: UniFFI `WorkHandle` with records
mirroring the core, and `ShellHandle.use_search_packs(paths)`; wasm `WorkHandle` with the core's
JSON shapes and TypeScript interfaces. Errors carry `kind` (`not_a_work_pack`, `not_found`,
`passage`, `storage`); no uniffi error field is named `message` or `cause`.
