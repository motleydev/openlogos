## The prototype

A fidelity prototype of interactions and typography, not the reader (B062 and later). Fixture
data only, labelled "Fixture:" under every text: the core's conformance fixtures through
`FixtureTexts` (WLC Genesis 1:1–2, public domain, right to left with points and cantillation;
Berean Standard Bible John 3:16–17, CC0), plus BSB Genesis 1:1–2 (CC0), the King James Version
John 3:16–17 (1769 Blayney text, public domain) and the Nestle 1904 Greek of John 3:16–17 copied
byte for byte from the B064 N1904 pack (`text` table, verse ids 44003016 and 44003017; MACULA
Greek, CC BY 4.0; B064's source entry number 73, which this branch's specs/sources.json does not
yet list, so the number is spelled out here to keep `just validate` honest) after the orchestrator
rejected a first version typed from memory.
No ESV, NIV or other restricted text. Cross references,
lexical data, search hits, collection items and vault paths are invented interaction shapes and
say so on screen. No network, account, store or model call exists in the code.

Views (specs/16 "Prototype and usability gate"), selected with the debug bar's View picker or
`-OPENLOGOS_VIEW`: Read; Three texts with drawers closed; Three texts + collection (drawer
open); Selection and word study (tappable words, action strip, Study drawer with Context →
Cross references → Word → Sources, cross-reference preview sheet); Search results; Margin
capture (snapshot preview; ink is P2); Obsidian handoff (preview → result → re-export).

Switches: `Layout A | B` (segmented control, or `-OPENLOGOS_LAYOUT`); State picker
(`normal, loading, empty, offline, unavailable, invalidQuery, readOnly, conflict`); simulated
viewport width (`Window`, 1440×900, 1024×768, 768×1024, 390×844); `Metrics` overlay; focus
mode from the workspace menu or `-OPENLOGOS_FOCUS 1`. Dynamic Type comes from the system
(`-UIPreferredContentSizeCategoryName`, as scripts/sim.sh's `OPENLOGOS_SIM_TEXT_SIZE`).

Compact width was produced with the viewport override on the iPad simulator, as instructed:
no iPhone simulator was created, booted or touched (the booted iPhone 17 Pro that belonged to
the user stayed as it was; every `simctl` call named the iPad's UDID or went through
scripts/sim.sh's recorded device). A 1024×768 viewport does not fit the portrait iPad (834×1210
points) upright, so the app draws it rotated by 90° and the script rotates the crop back; a
1440×900 viewport fits neither way and is drawn at scale 0.579 (the layout engine lays out at
the true size; the picture is smaller, the numbers are not).

Prototype rules checked by reading the code and the screenshots: Scripture text monochrome
(the only color is the active-pane underline and system control tint); every control has a
44-point minimum target (`ChromeMetrics.minimumTarget`); native text selection stays enabled on
the paragraphs (`.textSelection(.enabled)`), the tappable-word flow in the Selection view is a
stand-in for the native edit-menu extension that SwiftUI `Text` cannot express (specs/21 §3
says so); link state is a word plus a glyph, the active pane is weight plus underline plus the
`isSelected` trait; Hebrew lays out right to left inside the left-to-right workspace; at the
accessibility L size the shared bar becomes two rows, cantillation and Greek diacritics are
intact (`three-1024-A-largetype.png`), and three panes become two plus a tab.

## Layout A versus B

Both layouts share the pane model, drawers, capture and handoff; they differ only in where
global commands live (specs/21 §10).

- **A**: one 44-point shared bar (workspace menu, passage field, Search, collection indicator
  with name and count, More) and a 36-point inline identity row per pane.
- **B**: no bar; a 44-point-wide edge launcher (icons: Read, Search, Collect with count, Notes,
  Layouts, More; labels on hover or long press) which becomes a labelled 44-point bottom strip
  in compact width; a 44-point explicit header per pane; the active pane's header also holds the
  passage field, on its own 44-point row when the pane is narrower than 560 points (at 1024 and
  below with three panes it always is).

### Method

**Useful reading area** = the sum of the pane text regions (the scroll areas inside each
pane's identity row and the shared chrome, before text padding, drawers closed, three panes,
normal text size) in points². Chrome = bars, launcher, identity rows, tab rows, dividers. The
designed values come from `LayoutMetrics.designed`, which uses the same `ChromeMetrics`
constants the views lay out with; the `Metrics` overlay prints the designed value next to the
rendered value summed from the panes' `GeometryReader`s (`ReadingAreaKey`) and appends a JSON
line to `Documents/b004-metrics.jsonl` in the app container (`scripts/b004-shots.sh metrics`).
The `three-<width>-<layout>-metrics.png` screenshots carry the overlay.

**Duplicated commands** = the same global action reachable from two visible controls, or
listed in two menus, on one screen. Per-pane commands scoped to their pane (the pane menu on
each pane) are not duplicates. **Interactions** are counted from the screenshots and the code as
taps or clicks after the reader has already selected text, starting from the Read view with
three texts open.

### Reading of the comparison

A keeps more reading area at every width except that it is within 1% of B at 1440 and 1024;
B's advantage of having no bar is spent on taller per-pane headers and the passage row the
active pane needs once panes are narrower than 560 points, which is every three-pane layout up
to about 1700 points wide. A names the collection and its count in the bar; B shows a count on
an unlabeled icon in regular widths. B's compact strip is more discoverable than A's compact
bar (six labelled destinations against three), which argues for borrowing the labelled strip
for A's compact width rather than for adopting B. The starting hypothesis from specs/16 (favour
A) stands on these numbers; the decision is still B014's to validate with readers.

## Decisions to review

1. **Layout A as the starting point, with B's labelled compact strip considered for A's compact
   width.** The numbers above; validate in B014.
2. **B's passage field drops to its own row under 560 points of pane width.** The alternative,
   truncating the resource name to one letter beside the field (the first render,
   `three-1024-B` before 70e0d0c), hid the edition identity; the row costs 44 points of the
   active pane only.
3. **At accessibility type sizes a pane needs 480 points**, so 1024 shows two panes plus a tab.
   Spec 12's content-derived minimums say "increased where large text needs it"; 480 is the
   prototype's number, not a measured one.
4. **Compact drawers are in-viewport sheets in the prototype** so the simulator could render
   390-point layouts; the production reader uses the system sheet with medium and large detents
   (specs/21 §3, §5).
5. **The word-level selection in the Selection view is a flow of tappable words**, not the
   native selection; the native selection stays on every paragraph for Copy. The real reader
   extends `UIEditMenuInteraction` (specs/21 §3). Reviewers should not read the chips as a
   selection design.
6. **The orchestrator's mid-task corrections were applied in 49b62ea**: cleared Greek source
   (N1904 pack) instead of typed text; one contiguous range highlight with handles instead of
   per-word boxes, ragged right; the book label truncates before the reference; a single pane
   caps its measure (640 points at normal type, scaled with the text, centred); the word
   "Active" beside the accent underline so the active pane is not identified by color alone.
7. **"Active" as a word in the identity row** costs about 50 points of the row; at 1024 with
   three panes the active pane's book label truncates to "J…" (A) or to an ellipsis (B) while
   the reference stays whole. The word was chosen over a focus ring because it survives
   grayscale export and is read by VoiceOver without a custom label. The orchestrator's review
   preferred, when the row is crowded, dropping the book label entirely (reference only) or a
   cheaper cue (a leading dot plus the underline, with "Active" as the accessibility label).
   Left as is in the committed matrix to avoid a third re-shoot; the next iteration should use
   `ViewThatFits` to drop the book label before truncating it. Recorded here for B014.
