# Continuous reading: Scripture in context, one scroll from Genesis to Revelation

Revision 1, 2026-11-03 (B078). Owner direction, 2026-11-03: "When I type a Bible passage, I
want to be taken to the whole context, infinite scroll style, of scripture in context." This
file adds to specs/22 (the shell) and specs/21 (reading interactions); where they disagree
about reading position, this file wins.

## 1. Model

An **edition** is one ordered stream of chapters: Genesis 1:1 to Revelation 22:21 for a whole
Bible, Genesis to Malachi for the WLC, Matthew to Revelation for the SBLGNT. The **chapter**
is the unit of loading. A typed passage is a place in that stream, not a page: the tab opens
the passage's chapter, scrolls the passage to the top, marks the typed verses, and the reader
scrolls on into the chapters before and after without another command.

- **Chapter key**: `Book.C` (`John.3`), OSIS book ids.
- **Segment id**: `Book.C.V` (`John.3.16`), a Psalm title `Ps.3.0` in the English scheme, a
  bridge `Num.26.3-4`, a split verse `Num.26.5a` (specs/03, `SegmentTable`).
- **Book order**: canonical Protestant order for every edition for now. A pack may declare
  the BHS Tanakh order (`tanakh_bhs`, Torah, Prophets, Writings ending with Chronicles) and
  the core reads it in that order; no shipped edition does yet (decision D3 in
  docs/evidence/B078-core.md).
- **Versification**: every reference a tab stores is in its edition's own scheme. BSB is
  `eng` (English, Psalm titles as verse 0), SBLGNT and WLC are `org` (the original numbering:
  Hebrew chapter and verse numbers, a Psalm title as verse 1).

## 2. Canon and versification table

`openlogos_core::canon` holds the structure of both schemes for the 66 books: chapters per
book, verses per chapter, and the `eng` → `org` rows. It is generated by
`tools/gen_canon.py` from S51, the Copenhagen Alliance versification mappings derived from the
Paratext `.vrs` files (CC BY-SA 4.0), at a pinned commit; the generated file names the commit
and the input hashes. Structure only: no text.

Mapping follows the `.vrs` rule the table was written under: a verse no row covers keeps its
number, provided the target scheme has that verse. English Joel 2:28–32 is Hebrew 3:1–5,
English Joel 3 is Hebrew 4, English Malachi 4:1–6 is Hebrew 3:19–24, English Psalm 3:0 (the
title) is Hebrew 3:1 and 3:1 is Hebrew 3:2. Several English verses landing on one Hebrew verse
(Psalm 13:5–6 in Hebrew 13:6) each map `partial`; the Hebrew verse maps back exactly to all of
them. A verse the target lacks (English Acts 19:41, 2 Cor 13:14) is `unmapped`. The table
agrees with the hand-written `joel-malachi-chapter-numbering` and `psalm-9-10-numbering`
fixtures on every verse those fixtures' rows cover; the conformance suite checks it.

Deuterocanonical books are in S51 and are skipped until `BookId` gains them; the generator's
book list, the arrays and `BookOrder` grow together.

## 3. Reading API

The same calls on every host: Rust (`openlogos_text::reading::ReadingLibrary`), UniFFI
(`ReadingHandle`) and wasm (`ReadingHandle`), with the core's JSON field names.

| Call | Answer |
|---|---|
| `locate(resource_id, input)` | `{chapter_key, segment_id, reference, verse_start, verse_end, highlight_range, granularity}`. Verse ranges (`John 3:16–18`, across chapters `Jn 3:36–4:2`), the first of several ranges (`Rom 8:28, 31`), whole chapters (`Ps 23`, `Gen 1–3`: the first chapter, at its first segment, a titled Psalm at its title), whole books (`John`: chapter 1, verse 1). `highlight_range` only for verses. Errors: `invalid_reference`, `book_not_in_edition`, `chapter_out_of_range`, `verse_out_of_range` (“John 3 has no verse 37”), `unknown_resource`. |
| `chapter_neighbors(resource_id, chapter_key)` | `{chapter_key, prev, next}` across books and testaments (Malachi 4 → Matthew 1 in the BSB); `null` at either end of the edition (WLC Malachi 3 has no next). |
| `chapter_content(resource_id, chapter_key)` | A `ChapterBlock`: `heading {book_title (first chapter of a book only), chapter_label (“Chapter 3”, “Psalm 23”, the title of a one-chapter book), short_label (“Jn 3”)}`, `verses [{segment_id, number, last_number, label, text, paragraph_start, poetry, superscription, placeholder, tokens}]`, `prev`, `next`, `placeholder`. |
| `reference_for_segment(segment_id)` | The verse a segment starts at, OSIS; `null` for a segment that names no verse. |
| `chunk_for` / `supply_chunk` | A file pack's chapter location `{path, offset, length, sha256}`; the bytes handed back, checked against the hash (§4). |
| `editions()`, `schemes()` | The installed editions; resource id → scheme for the shell. |

Which segment is at the top of the viewport is the host's measurement; the core turns it into
a reference.

## 4. Text pack format (B078 writes the reader; B071 writes the packs)

A pack is a directory of static files, so a browser can read it with plain fetches:

```
manifest.json          schemas/text-pack.schema.json
chapters/John/3.json   one chunk per chapter (default layout), or
chapters.bin           every chunk concatenated; each chapter found by offset and length
```

`manifest.json`: `format` `openlogos-text-pack`, `format_version` `1.x` (a reader refuses
another major), `resource_id`, `content_revision`, `title`, `name`, `language` (BCP 47),
`direction`, `scheme`, `book_order`, `license`, `source`, `fixture_only`, `complete`, and
`books` in reading order, each `{book, title, chapters: [{chapter, first_verse, last_verse,
path, offset?, length, sha256}]}`. Book titles are in the edition's language; the heading
shows them. A chunk is UTF-8 JSON `{format: "openlogos-text-pack-chapter", resource_id,
chapter_key, verses: [ChapterVerse]}` with the text byte-exact, never normalized. Paths are
relative, without `.` or `..` segments.

Every chunk is checked against its manifest SHA-256 before use; the manifest's own SHA-256 is
what the device catalog records (B068). Natively the core reads chunks from disk
(`ReadingHandle.open_pack(directory)`); in a browser the page installs the manifest
(`addPackManifest`), fetches the chunk `chunkFor` names (a whole file, or `Range:
bytes=offset-(offset+length-1)` of the bundle) and hands the bytes back (`supplyChunk`);
`chapterContent` throws `chunk_not_loaded` until then. `PackWriter` writes the format; B071's
importer calls it. The three fixture packs under `crates/conformance/fixtures/packs/` are
written from the conformance text fixtures only (one chapter each; WLC as a bundle).

## 5. Placeholder editions

Until B071's packs are installed, the hosts read **placeholder editions** generated in-process
from the canon table (`ReadingHandle.placeholder()`): BSB (66 books, `eng`), SBLGNT (New
Testament, `org`), WLC (Old Testament, `org`). Rules:

- Every verse is a short line that says what it is, in the edition's script, and never reads
  as Scripture: `Placeholder verse — John 3:15`, `Placeholder title — Psalm 3`, `Κείμενο
  κράτησης θέσης — 3:17`, `טקסט ממלא מקום — 1:3`. Each carries `placeholder: true`, and the
  block's `placeholder` is true when any verse is.
- Verses the conformance fixtures hold (BSB John 3:16–17, SBLGNT John 3:16, WLC Genesis
  1:1–2) are overlaid with their real text and tokens, `placeholder: false`.
- Layout is a placeholder's too: a paragraph every five verses in prose, one line per verse in
  Job 3:1–42:6, Psalms, Proverbs, Song of Songs and Lamentations, Psalm titles marked
  `superscription` (verse 0 in `eng`; in `org` the verses up to the one the English title maps
  to, so Psalm 51:1–2).
- A host shows that the text is a placeholder (specs/21 §11 placeholder state) and never lets
  a placeholder verse be quoted, copied as Scripture, highlighted into a collection or sent to
  the research assistant.

## 6. Position, header and linking (crates/workspaces)

- **The tab's passage follows the reading position.** `tab.record_scroll` (quiet: no undo, no
  history entry) takes the segment at the top of the viewport (and optionally the host's own
  `reference`); a Reading tab's `reference` becomes that verse unless the tab's stored verse
  range already holds it (scrolling inside `John 3:16–18` keeps the range). The identity row
  (`Jn 4:7`) and the tab label (`BSB Jn 4`) follow. The tab's current history visit is updated
  in place, so Back returns to where the reader was; Back and Forward stacks change only on
  explicit navigation. A scroll that moved a reference bumps the shell revision (labels and
  palette rows changed); a scroll within a verse does not.
- **Linked tabs follow through versification.** The scrolled tab drives its link group, and,
  when it is the active tab, every follow-mode tab (specs/21 §2). Each follower's reference is
  mapped from the driver's scheme into its own (`Shell::set_resource_schemes`, from the
  reading handle's `schemes()`): BSB Joel 2:29 moves a linked WLC tab to Joel 3:2. A verse
  with no counterpart lands on the nearest earlier verse of its chapter that has one
  (`nearest`); without registered schemes followers take the driver's reference as written.
  A follower already at its mapped verse does not move, so a host that reports the scroll it
  was told to make changes nothing. Followers get their scroll anchor set to the mapped
  verse; they are not given history entries.
- **Every moved tab is in the result.** `Effects.positions` (`ShellOutcome.positions`) lists
  the driver first, then each follower: `{workspace_id, tab_id, driver, scrolled, reference,
  segment_id, reference_label, chapter_label, highlight_range, mapping}`, `mapping` one of
  `same`, `unregistered`, `exact`, `partial`, `nearest`, `unmapped`.
- **Navigation marks the typed verses.** `nav.navigate` stores `highlight_range` (OSIS) on the
  tab when the reference names verses, and on each follower its mapped range; a chapter or
  book navigation marks nothing (and neither do its followers). The mark stays until the next
  navigation of that tab, Back and Forward included; scrolling does not clear it.
  `tab.open_edition` opens the comparison at the reference mapped into the edition's scheme.

## 7. Host behaviour expected

- **Virtualized chapter blocks.** A reading tab renders a window of chapter blocks around the
  position (the chapter in view and one or two each side), loading neighbours through
  `chapter_neighbors` / `prev` / `next` as the reader nears either end, and releasing blocks
  far away. Never render a whole Bible.
- **Prepend without a jump.** Inserting the previous chapter above keeps the verse under the
  reader's eye exactly where it was (anchor the scroll position to a segment, not to pixels).
- **Sticky header.** The chapter heading of the block in view sticks at the top of the tab
  (`short_label`), and book titles head a book's first chapter.
- **Flash and mark.** On navigation, scroll `segment_id` to the top, flash the
  `highlight_range` verses once and keep a quiet mark on them until the next navigation.
- **Report position quietly.** When scrolling settles, send `tab.record_scroll` with the
  segment at the top; update the identity row and tab label from `positions` without
  rebuilding the reading column.
- **Linked scroll.** For each follower in `positions` with `scrolled: true`, scroll it to its
  `segment_id` (loading that chapter if needed) without animation, and do not report that
  programmatic scroll as the reader's.
- **Placeholders are visible** (§5) and an edition without a book shows specs/21's
  not-in-this-edition state for that tab.

Budgets: a chapter opens in ≤ 100 ms end to end (specs/22 §7); `locate`, `chapter_content` and
`chapter_neighbors` stay far below 5 ms in the core (`just bench-shell`).
